# Author-only answer keys, solutions, and hardening gates

Do **not** expose this file, its answers, or its distractors to the model being tested. Before use, replace each image brief with a real, allowed image that actually contains the specified discriminating evidence. ## 1–5 Histopathology

### 1. Invasive front
**GTFA:** Irregular tumour nests infiltrating desmoplastic stroma

**Required image evidence:** At least two angular epithelial nests must lie beyond the duct boundary in reactive, collagenous stroma; the image must also contain smooth retraction clefts around a separate in-situ nest.

**Solution:** Step 1: Locate the nests that are irregularly distributed in desmoplastic stroma, not the nests rimmed by clear clefts. Step 2: Confirm that these nests lack a continuous myoepithelial/basement-membrane boundary. Step 3: Stromal infiltration establishes invasion. **Final answer: Irregular tumour nests infiltrating desmoplastic stroma.**

**Distractors:** Retraction artifact; comedo necrosis; apocrine change; ductal carcinoma in situ; stromal edema.

### 2. Mitotic figure
**GTFA:** Mitotic figure

**Required image evidence:** One cell must have sharply discrete chromosomes aligned centrally, with no rounded condensed apoptotic fragment or perinuclear halo.

**Solution:** Step 1: Identify the cell with individual condensed chromosomes arranged in a plate. Step 2: Note that apoptosis produces a rounded pyknotic body and fragmentation, not an organized chromosome plate. Step 3: The organized chromosomal arrangement is mitosis. **Final answer: Mitotic figure.**

**Distractors:** Apoptotic body; pyknosis; crush artifact; melanin pigment; stain precipitate.

### 3. Granuloma subtype
**GTFA:** Caseating granuloma

**Required image evidence:** The centre must be amorphous, eosinophilic, acellular, and granular, circled by epithelioid histiocytes and giant cells; avoid a merely pale fold.

**Solution:** Step 1: Inspect the centre rather than the peripheral rim. Step 2: It lacks intact nuclei and shows granular acellular necrosis. Step 3: Central caseous necrosis defines a caseating granuloma. **Final answer: Caseating granuloma.**

**Distractors:** Noncaseating granuloma; suppurative granuloma; foreign-body granuloma; fibrinoid necrosis; tissue fold.

### 4. IHC compartment
**GTFA:** Nuclear

**Required image evidence:** Brown DAB must be confined within haematoxylin-counterstained tumour nuclei, while macrophage pigment remains granular and cytoplasmic.

**Solution:** Step 1: Identify viable tumour cells rather than pigmented macrophages. Step 2: Localize brown chromogen relative to each tumour-cell boundary and nucleus. Step 3: The staining overlaps nuclei. **Final answer: Nuclear.**

**Distractors:** Cytoplasmic; membranous; extracellular; Golgi-pattern; nonspecific pigment.

### 5. Thyroid nuclear feature
**GTFA:** Nuclear grooves

**Required image evidence:** Multiple overlapping lesional nuclei must show longitudinal indentations; do not use an image containing only chromatin pallor.

**Solution:** Step 1: Focus on the internal contour of lesional nuclei. Step 2: Observe repeated linear invaginations across nuclei. Step 3: These are nuclear grooves. **Final answer: Nuclear grooves.**

**Distractors:** Keratinization; coarse chromatin clumping; nucleolar prominence; squamous pearl formation; intracytoplasmic mucin.

## 6–10 Cytology and cytogenetics

### 6. Pap smear lesion grade
**GTFA:** High-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion

**Required image evidence:** A crowded group must have scant cytoplasm, high N:C ratios, irregular nuclear membranes, and hyperchromasia; include mature superficial cells separately.

**Solution:** Step 1: Compare nucleus size with the visible cytoplasmic rim in the atypical cluster. Step 2: The cluster has uniformly high N:C ratios and irregular hyperchromatic nuclei. Step 3: This pattern supports HSIL. **Final answer: High-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion.**

**Distractors:** Low-grade squamous intraepithelial lesion; atypical squamous cells of undetermined significance; atrophy; reactive metaplasia; squamous-cell carcinoma.

### 7. Koilocytosis
**GTFA:** Sharply demarcated halo with a thickened irregular cytoplasmic rim

**Required image evidence:** The target mature squamous cell must show a crisp perinuclear halo and dense, irregular surrounding cytoplasm; diffuse glycogen clearing must be visible in another cell.

**Solution:** Step 1: Compare the edge of perinuclear clearing in the target and control-like cells. Step 2: The target clearing has a crisp edge and thick irregular rim. Step 3: That morphology is diagnostic koilocytosis. **Final answer: Sharply demarcated halo with a thickened irregular cytoplasmic rim.**

**Distractors:** Diffuse cytoplasmic clearing without a rim; nuclear pyknosis; orangeophilia; ciliary tuft; streaming artifact.

### 8. Metaphase class
**GTFA:** Euploid diploid

**Required image evidence:** A fully scorable spread must contain 46 countable chromosomes after excluding debris and a nonchromosomal edge artifact; no extra homologue group.

**Solution:** Step 1: Count only chromosome-shaped objects in the central spread. Step 2: Exclude debris and confirm 46 chromosomes. Step 3: Forty-six chromosomes is a diploid euploid complement. **Final answer: Euploid diploid.**

**Distractors:** Trisomy 21; monosomy X; near-tetraploid; triploidy; unbalanced translocation.

### 9. FISH pattern
**GTFA:** Target amplification

**Required image evidence:** At least 20 intact isolated nuclei must show clustered or numerous red target signals with two green control signals; exclude truncated nuclei.

**Solution:** Step 1: Restrict assessment to intact, non-overlapping nuclei. Step 2: Compare red target signal number with green control number. Step 3: Excess clustered red signals indicate amplification. **Final answer: Target amplification.**

**Distractors:** Target deletion; balanced diploid pattern; polysomy of both probes; signal truncation; hybridization failure.

### 10. FNA background
**GTFA:** Thick ropy colloid

**Required image evidence:** The background must contain dense, smooth, viscous-looking magenta-blue colloid distinct from granular necrosis.

**Solution:** Step 1: Inspect the extracellular background between follicular sheets. Step 2: It contains thick, cohesive, rope-like colloid. Step 3: Ropy colloid supports a benign colloid nodule. **Final answer: Thick ropy colloid.**

**Distractors:** Tumour necrosis; tigroid background; mucin; amyloid; blood clot.

## 11–15 Hematology

### 11. Schistocyte
**GTFA:** Keratocyte

**Required image evidence:** Include a fragmented red cell with two horn-like projections and a central remnant, plus separate bite and crenated cells.

**Solution:** Step 1: Identify the irregular red cell with two pointed horn-like ends. Step 2: Exclude the evenly spiculated crenated cell and single-notched bite cell. Step 3: The horned fragmentation pattern is a keratocyte, a schistocyte form. **Final answer: Keratocyte.**

**Distractors:** Echinocyte; bite cell; spherocyte; target cell; elliptocyte.

### 12. Malaria stage
**GTFA:** Schizont

**Required image evidence:** One erythrocyte must contain several discrete chromatin dots/merozoites arranged around central pigment; nearby platelets may overlap other cells.

**Solution:** Step 1: Locate the parasite-containing erythrocyte with multiple internal bodies. Step 2: The bodies are organized within one red cell around pigment. Step 3: Multiple merozoites define a schizont. **Final answer: Schizont.**

**Distractors:** Ring trophozoite; gametocyte; platelet overlay; Howell–Jolly body; stain precipitate.

### 13. Blast lineage
**GTFA:** Myeloid

**Required image evidence:** A genuine straight, reddish-purple needle-shaped Auer rod must lie in blast cytoplasm, not across multiple cells.

**Solution:** Step 1: Find the cytoplasmic needle-like inclusion in the blast. Step 2: Verify that it is bounded by one cell rather than a stain streak. Step 3: An Auer rod supports myeloid lineage. **Final answer: Myeloid.**

**Distractors:** Lymphoid; plasma-cell; megakaryocytic; erythroid; monocytic only.

### 14. RBC inclusion
**GTFA:** Howell–Jolly body

**Required image evidence:** The inclusion must be solitary, round, deeply basophilic, and within the erythrocyte; stippling must appear as multiple granules elsewhere.

**Solution:** Step 1: Examine number and shape of the blue inclusion. Step 2: It is a single dense round body rather than diffuse multiple dots. Step 3: This is a Howell–Jolly body. **Final answer: Howell–Jolly body.**

**Distractors:** Basophilic stippling; Pappenheimer body; Heinz body; malaria ring; stain precipitate.

### 15. Platelet estimate
**GTFA:** Adequate

**Required image evidence:** Across at least 10 monolayer fields, show roughly 8–15 platelets per oil field, excluding a feather-edge clump.

**Solution:** Step 1: Assess only the monolayer fields. Step 2: Exclude the edge clump and count representative platelet density. Step 3: The density falls in the adequate estimate range. **Final answer: Adequate.**

**Distractors:** Decreased; increased; pseudothrombocytopenic; platelet clumping; giant-platelet syndrome.

## 16–20 Fluorescence microscopy

### 16. Cargo localisation
**GTFA:** Punctate colocalization with lysosomes

**Required image evidence:** In non-saturated cells, red puncta must coincide spatially with green puncta in both channels; saturation should be limited to a different region.

**Solution:** Step 1: Exclude saturated pixels and inspect separate channels. Step 2: Match discrete red puncta to green lysosomal puncta. Step 3: Repeated channel overlap demonstrates punctate colocalization. **Final answer: Punctate colocalization with lysosomes.**

**Distractors:** Diffuse cytosolic separation; nuclear localization; saturation-only overlap; membrane-only signal; autofluorescence.

### 17. Nuclear translocation
**GTFA:** Nuclear

**Solution:** Step 1: Select cells with intact DAPI nuclei. Step 2: Compare factor signal to the DAPI-defined nuclear region. Step 3: The predominant signal lies in nuclei. **Final answer: Nuclear.**

**Distractors:** Cytoplasmic; membranous; mitochondrial; extracellular; nonspecific dead-cell uptake.

### 18. Z-stack equator

**Solution:** Step 1: Compare the projected cross-sectional width in all planes. Step 2: Plane 2 has the largest continuous profile. Step 3: The maximal profile lies closest to the equator. **Final answer: Plane 2.**

**Distractors:** Plane 1; Plane 3; all planes equally equatorial; surface plane; no plane intersects the spheroid.

### 19. Photobleaching fraction
**GTFA:** 0.32

**Required image evidence:** The higher-starting trace must read 250 arbitrary units pre-bleach and 80 at the final time after using the graph’s nonzero baseline correctly.

**Solution:** Step 1: Bind the higher initial trace to its legend style. Step 2: Read 250 pre-bleach and 80 at the final time. Step 3: Compute 80/250 = 0.32. **Final answer: 0.32.**

**Distractors:** 0.20; 0.25; 0.40; 0.68; 0.80.

### 20. Dead-cell dye
**GTFA:** Cells with lost plasma-membrane integrity

**Solution:** Step 1: Locate dye-positive nuclei within cell outlines. Step 2: The dye enters only cells with compromised membranes. Step 3: It marks loss of membrane integrity. **Final answer: Cells with lost plasma-membrane integrity.**

**Distractors:** Lower invariant band; darkest band; band nearest the well; broad smear; ladder band.
